# Code of Conduct

## Why this exists

Most people using and contributing to this project are students, many of them new to Model UN and
some writing their first pull request. That shapes what is expected here: patience with beginners is
not optional politeness, it is the point.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless
of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

**A note specific to this project:** it is about international politics, and its users represent
countries whose real-world governments they may personally agree with or oppose. Arguing a country's
position in committee is roleplay. Attacking a contributor for the country they were assigned, for
where they are actually from, or for the politics of any state — that is not roleplay, and it is not
welcome here. Keep the debate in the committee room.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the other person is acting in good faith and is possibly fifteen years old.
- Give feedback on the code, not the person. "This crashes when the list is empty" — not "did you
  even test this".
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change without it being a judgement of you.
- Take responsibility, apologise, and move on when you get something wrong.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention of any kind
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information — including someone's prepared competition material,
  transcripts, or personal details — without their explicit permission
- Deliberately introducing malicious code, or attempting to use a pull request to exfiltrate data or
  credentials from anyone running this project
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments — and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behaviour privately to the maintainer through
[GitHub's private reporting](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
or by opening a GitHub security advisory on this repository. All reports will be reviewed and
investigated promptly and fairly, and the reporter's privacy will be respected.

Maintainers may edit, delete or reject comments, commits, code, issues and pull requests that violate
this Code of Conduct, and will explain why when they do. Depending on severity, consequences range
from a private warning to a permanent ban from the project.
